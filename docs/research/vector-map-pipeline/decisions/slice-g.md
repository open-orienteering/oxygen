| 2026-11-13 | G2: the patch schema and the effective document | **Questions decided by the user (the planner's recommendations); the rest decided in the row** (`map-patches.md` §1–2). | 2026-11-13 | G22: GPX parsing and track geometry | **Decided by the G22 planner and session; new, the research has none beyond 04 §7.** `gpsTrack` in `@oxygen/shared`, normative text `map-gps-tracks.md` §2–3. Tracks are columnar WGS 84 — lat / lng as 1e-7° integers quantised at parse, time as epoch ms UTC (absolute, not ZeroTime-relative), elevation to 0.1 m, a column null when no point has a value — the shape G24 stores verbatim. `parseGpx` streams over `import/xml.ts` `parseXml` (GPX 1.0 / 1.1, namespace prefixes stripped, one track per segment, `rte` untimed, waypoints counted, zoneless times read as UTC by a regex, invalid points and < 2-point segments skipped and counted, no caps; errors `malformed` / `not-gpx` / `no-points`). `projectTrack` is index-aligned with the points (a non-finite point throws `not-projectable` instead of being dropped), points outside the header bounds kept and counted; `unprojectTrack` added for G23. Smoothing: equal-interval resample at ≤ 2 m, triangular weights 1-2-3-2-1 (K = floor(window / 2 / step)) with a window that shrinks symmetrically to pin the ends; the noise property is "under two thirds of the RMS" (the planner's 60 % is below what resampling a noisy arc allows; the kernel's floor is 0.48). Douglas–Peucker at 0.1 mm against segments, iterative. Resample, smoothing and simplification use only + − × ÷ and √ (no `Math.hypot`), so they are bit-identical across engines; projection is not (proj4) and is made reproducible across devices by quantisation only. Trim is `[start, end)`; a split shares its point. Thrown typed error classes, as `XmlParseError`; the first `ele` / `time` of a point wins; coordinates are plain decimals; impossible dates are unreadable (spec-review findings). **Deviates from 04 §7**: tracks are projected to paper **mm** by `mapGeo.wgs84ToPaper` (04 says paper µm by a `geoToPaper`), on every use from stored WGS 84 rather than "once". Ground metres = paper mm × mapScale / 1000, ignoring the grid scale factor. | The row says paper mm, and mm is the editor's overlay unit (`geoToMm`); storing WGS 84 (slice G split) means projection cannot be once. Quantising at parse makes a parsed and a stored track the same input, so averaging is reproducible. Index alignment is what G27's handles rest on. For G24's and G27's planners: the `TrackPoints` shape and the `[start, end)` trim are this row's interface. |
