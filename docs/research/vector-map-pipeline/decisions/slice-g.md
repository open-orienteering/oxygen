| 2026-11-13 | G22: GPX parsing and track geometry | **Decided by the G22 planner and session; new, the research has none beyond 04 §7.** `gpsTrack` in `@oxygen/shared`, normative text `map-gps-tracks.md` §2–3. Tracks are columnar WGS 84 — lat / lng as 1e-7° integers quantised at parse, time as epoch ms UTC (absolute, not ZeroTime-relative), elevation to 0.1 m, a column null when no point has a value — the shape G24 stores verbatim. `parseGpx` streams over `import/xml.ts` `parseXml` (GPX 1.0 / 1.1, namespace prefixes stripped, one track per segment, `rte` untimed, waypoints counted, zoneless times read as UTC by a regex, invalid points and < 2-point segments skipped and counted, no caps; errors `malformed` / `not-gpx` / `no-points`). `projectTrack` is index-aligned with the points (a non-finite point throws `not-projectable` instead of being dropped), points outside the header bounds kept and counted; `unprojectTrack` added for G23. Smoothing: equal-interval resample at ≤ 2 m, triangular weights 1-2-3-2-1 (K = floor(window / 2 / step)) with a window that shrinks symmetrically to pin the ends; the noise property is "under two thirds of the RMS" (the planner's 60 % is below what resampling a noisy arc allows; the kernel's floor is 0.48). Douglas–Peucker at 0.1 mm against segments, iterative. Resample, smoothing and simplification use only + − × ÷ and √ (no `Math.hypot`), so they are bit-identical across engines; projection is not (proj4) and is made reproducible across devices by quantisation only. Trim is `[start, end)`; a split shares its point. Thrown typed error classes, as `XmlParseError`; the first `ele` / `time` of a point wins; coordinates are plain decimals; impossible dates are unreadable (spec-review findings). **Deviates from 04 §7**: tracks are projected to paper **mm** by `mapGeo.wgs84ToPaper` (04 says paper µm by a `geoToPaper`), on every use from stored WGS 84 rather than "once". Ground metres = paper mm × mapScale / 1000, ignoring the grid scale factor. | The row says paper mm, and mm is the editor's overlay unit (`geoToMm`); storing WGS 84 (slice G split) means projection cannot be once. Quantising at parse makes a parsed and a stored track the same input, so averaging is reproducible. Index alignment is what G27's handles rest on. For G24's and G27's planners: the `TrackPoints` shape and the `[start, end)` trim are this row's interface. |

| 2026-11-13 | G23: averaging several passes | **Questions 1–4 answered by the user at G23's planning (2026-11-13); the rest decided by the G23 planner and session; new, the research has none.** `averageTracks(passes, opts)` in `gpsTrack`, normative text `map-gps-tracks.md` §4, on projected tracks in and a projected line out (the caller stores it with `unprojectTrack`); result `{ line, singlePass, reference, reversed }`, `singlePass` as `TrackRange` point indices of the line, `reversed` per input pass (added at spec review so the ends tie-break is observable and tested); `gpsTrack` exports only `averageTracks`, its two types and the two defaults (`unitNormals` stays module-internal, tested directly). **Refines the fixed method of slice G "Decided in this split" (user):** (Q1) every pass is first turned to a canonical direction — its point list or the reverse, whichever is lexicographically smaller — so reversing any pass or reordering gives the same bytes and the result's direction is arbitrary but fixed; (Q2a) a match is the nearest point in the **first run** of consecutive segments within 15 m after the previous match, not the nearest of the whole remainder, so a loop crossing itself does not cross-match; (Q2b) a match at a pass's first or last point votes only within one step along the tangent, so a pass that stops does not smear its end over 15 m; (Q3) the other passes are orientated **by evidence** — matched both ways, the direction with more voting samples kept, the ends rule breaking ties — so a closed loop skied the other way round is averaged; (Q4) G24 stores the ranges (`single_pass JSONB NULL`, G24 row amended). **Decided by the session:** the upper median (index `floor(n / 2)`, the longer of two) — the row's half-pass test needs the longer pass as reference; the tie goes to the smallest list among the passes of exactly the median length (sorting by (length, list) and taking the median index picks the wrong one of two equal passes); a sample with fewer than two votes is single-pass, one with none stays, a lone vote in a later iteration moves the sample onto that pass (in the first iteration the lone vote is the reference's 0, so "stays" as fixed); the second iteration matches every pass, the first reference included, and keeps the reference choice and orientation; tangents by central difference with one-sided ends and a forward/backward fallback at an exact hairpin; passes matched unresampled; plain forward scan, no spatial index (with a half-covering pass at 1 m fixes: 10 km well under a second, 50 km 3.3 s, 100 km 16 s — G27 runs it off the main thread or caps the input); `RangeError`s as in `geometry.ts`. **Untested by choice:** "ties to the earliest" in a run (two exactly equidistant points of one run; hard to construct, the review's mutation survived). **Known property, documented:** a pass leaving the others at an angle votes with its departing stretch for up to 15 m, so two passes bulge there; with three the median rejects it. | The row's own tests (a reversed pass, a half pass, a loop) are not met by the literal six steps; each refinement is the smallest change that meets one. Canonical direction costs nothing, since a stored average is untimed. For G24's and G27's planners: `single_pass` is part of an average's row and shifts with its trim. |
