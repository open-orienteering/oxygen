| 2026-11-07 | P7: crossing rings in the GL fill | `map-rendering.md` §5.2 recorded "rings that cross are earcut's failure mode and drawn as earcut decides (the reference raster is the oracle)". The white-out outside's shape crosses the window ring whenever it reaches the frame, and `triangulateEvenOdd`'s first-vertex containment probe then filled the shape (the whole frame white) or handed earcut a crossing hole (a white slash; user 2026-11-07, `docs/bugfix-whiteout-layout.md`). The rule is narrowed: a ring crossing or touching a **convex** ring is resolved before earcut. Two rings (every white-out outside) are triangulated exactly by convex clips of earcut triangles — the window outside the shape from a box with the shape as an untouching hole, the shape outside the window from its own triangles — so the GL fill equals the PDF's and the raster's even-odd; a first version that made the shape's inside part a hole of the window failed the spec review (Sutherland–Hodgman's bridges and holes touching the outer at fractional coordinates, which earcut drops). More rings (no producer emits them) are resolved best effort into parts inside and outside, probed at a free boundary point. Only two non-convex crossing rings remain earcut's. Cost (the crossing test returns at once for a ring wholly inside, else bounds-filters the edges): a 5 000-vertex ring round 100 convex holes 7.6 ms against 6.4 ms before, a 64-vertex hull inside its window 13.5 µs against 12.4 µs. Decided by the P7 session: the fix is in the GL backend (the stream is correct even-odd, print and the raster already exact; clipping in the shared layer would flatten the hull's Béziers out of the PDF); the shape's parts outside the window are filled as even-odd says (beyond the frame they are clipped away on screen as in print). Fills without a crossing triangulate as before (goldens unchanged).
