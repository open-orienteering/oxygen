## What the code says today (the planner's seams)

- **Touch never reaches a tool's press.** `MapViewer.tsx` `onTouchStart`
  (l. 1555–1568) runs `armEditorTargetRef` (sites, numbers, legs) and never
  `tool.onPointerDown`. The only tool press on touch is the tap-on-object
  path in `onTouchEnd` (l. 1819–1822). The mouse path (`handleMouseDown`,
  l. 1294) offers the tool first. A bend handle sits on the leg, so a
  tablet press on it becomes a leg click, and a grip (off the leg) pans.
- **The stem and loupe are wired to control drags only.** The touch
  handle offset and the loupe come only from `editorDragPos` +
  `editorDragTouch` + `touchFinger` (l. 2491–2526). `glLoupe` →
  `VectorMapLayer.loupe` → `engine.renderInset(loupeView(...))` doesn't
  depend on the pointer type. `loupePlacement(finger, box)`
  (`lib/editor/touch.ts`) hard-codes the 48 px lift.
- **Bend presses.** `selectBinding` (`bindings.ts` l. 203–215) takes bend
  and grip presses at `HANDLE_PX` = 7 px. Its `onPointerDrag` returns
  nothing, so the viewer never learns the snapped point the tool placed.
  `onPinch.start` already uses `2 * HANDLE_PX` = 14 px on touch: the
  precedent for a coarse reach.
- **Leg metres.** `EditorPage.tsx`:
  - `legEditable = !!selectedLeg && !isMobile` (l. 425; a tablet is
    already editable);
  - `legMeters` (l. 257–264) reads the stored `item.leg.bends` and ignores
    `legDraft`; `totalMeters` and CourseMode's `km` derive from it;
  - the status length is `courseChecks.lengthM ?? totalMeters`, which
    already follows the F21 draft, one round trip behind.
- **The band.** `course/layer.ts` l. 129 appends
  `{ mode: "clip", symbols: "all" }`. `FillPass` has no opacity, WebGL2
  fills draw with blend off, and the PDF has no ExtGState. `whiteOpacity`
  is exposed in `pages/editor/modes/LegSection.tsx`
  (`editor-leg-corridor-opacity`, hint `legCorridorOpacityHint`: "The
  white band is drawn opaque for now").
- **E2E.** `e2e/helpers/touch.ts` dispatches synthetic `TouchEvent`s.
  `e2e/map-viewer-gestures.spec.ts` shows how to get a tablet inside the
  desktop project (a coarse pointer at ≥ 640 px gives the desktop
  layout):
  `test.use({ hasTouch: true, isMobile: true, viewport })`.
- **Stale doc.** `docs/features.md` l. 79 still mentions the legacy
  editor's mouse loupe (`MapLoupe`, retired in E4b). F24 fixes it.

## F23: bend and grip drags on touch (tablet)

**Scope.** On a coarse pointer with the desktop layout (a tablet), one
finger on the selected leg's bend handle or grip drags it:
- the dragged point rides 48 px above the finger on the dashed stem
  (`editor-touch-handle`), with the D9b loupe above it;
- the bend snaps (§6.2), and the course layer follows live (F21's draft,
  unchanged);
- the release writes once, and a tap focuses the bend.

There's no Alt on touch, so the bend row's **Smooth / Corner** stays the
switch (F16). A bend drag moves the bend with its handles, and a grip drag
keeps the pair mirrored (F18's default). Unchanged: the phone stays
read-only (answer 1), and Field mode shows no selected leg on any device.

Not in scope:
- touch drags of course-object vertices and grips (Pending);
- the mouse loupe (F24);
- live metres (F25).

**Acceptance criteria.**
- **Starting the gesture.** A press within **14 CSS px**
  (`2 * HANDLE_PX`) of a bend handle (or, failing that, a grip) of the
  selected leg on a coarse pointer starts the gesture, and the leg click
  under it does not fire (`course-editor.md` §6.7 "Pressing order", §10
  "Gestures", new row).
- **Moving.** Past 3 px of finger travel:
  - the bend is drawn at `liftedPoint` (48 px above the finger), snapped,
    on the stem;
  - it's released where drawn (§10 "Dragging a control by touch", applied
    to bends and grips).
- **Grips.** A grip drag moves the handle by the lifted pointer's offset
  from the press, with the pair mirrored.
- **The loupe** (`editor-loupe`, 120 px, ×2.5, 16 px above the handle,
  flipped and clamped as today) shows during the drag and goes on release.
- **Live redraw and writes.** The layer redraws per move through
  `onBendsDraft`, and the release writes once. A second finger or
  `touchcancel` cancels: `onPointerUp(null)` drops the draft with no
  write.
- **A tap** on a handle without movement focuses it, with no write.
- **The phone:** `editor-leg` `data-readonly="true"`, no handles. The
  existing phone test stays green.
- **Tests:**
  - unit, `bindings.test.ts`, new describe "V on a selected leg: touch
    (F23)":
    - a touch press takes a bend at 10 px and declines it at 16 px;
    - it takes a grip at 10 px;
    - a touch press on an object target is declined;
    - `onPointerDrag` returns the snapped bend or the grip point;
    - a tap focuses;
  - E2E, `course-editor.spec.ts`, new describe "F23: bend drags on a
    tablet", one test with two gestures, under
    `test.use({ hasTouch: true, isMobile: true, viewport: { width: 1024, height: 768 } })`:
    - `data-layout="desktop"`; tap the leg, then *Add bend here*;
    - `touchStart` on the handle and `touchMove` ×6:
      `editor-touch-handle` and `editor-loupe` are visible, and the UI
      handle sits 48 px above the finger;
    - one `course.setStructure` on `touchEnd`, with the stored bend within
      0.5 mm of the lifted point;
    - then a grip touch-drag changes the stored handles and keeps them
      mirrored.

**Steps.**
1. **`bindings.ts` + its test, test first.**
   - Add `ToolPointer.touch?: boolean` and `EditorToolBinding.touchPress?:
     boolean` (a binding opts in to one-finger presses).
   - `selectBinding` sets `touchPress: !!leg`. When `p.touch`, it uses a
     reach of `2 * HANDLE_PX` and takes **only** bend and grip presses
     (false for objects, so the tap-to-select path of `onTouchEnd` keeps
     working).
   - `onPointerDrag` returns `Pt | void`: the snapped bend for `bend`, the
     grip point for `bend-grip`, the vertex or grip point for `object`
     (vertex) and `grip`, and nothing otherwise.
2. **`MapViewer.tsx`:**
   - widen the `EditorToolBinding.onPointerDrag` type, and add state
     `toolDragPos: Pt | null` and `toolDragTouch`;
   - `onTouchStart` (one finger, tool not exclusive, `tool.touchPress`)
     offers `tool.onPointerDown({ ...toolPointer, touch: true })`
     **before** `armEditorTargetRef`;
   - `onTouchMove`, past 3 px, calls
     `onPointerDrag(screenToMapMm(liftedPoint(finger)), { shift: false, alt: false })`
     and stores the returned point as `toolDragPos`;
   - `onTouchEnd` calls `onPointerUp(lifted mm)` after a move, or
     `onPointerUp(press mm)` for a tap;
   - a second finger, `touchcancel`, `hadMultiTouchRef` or the 400 ms
     suppression calls `onPointerUp(null)` before any `onPinch.start`;
   - the stem and loupe block takes
     `dragPoint = editorDragPos ?? toolDragPos`, with the matching touch
     flag.
3. **E2E:** append the describe after the F3 phone describe, and prove in
   the same test that the context is coarse (the stem appears).
4. **Docs:**
   - `course-editor.md`: §6.7 "Phone" (the tablet drags by touch); §10
     (the gestures table row and "Loupe"); the Tests table; trim the
     Pending "Bend drags on touch";
   - `map-rendering.md` §5.5 "Loupe";
   - `docs/mobile-layout.md` l. 77;
   - `features.md`;
   - a row in `decisions/slice-f.md`;
   - the SLICES row.

**Decided in this plan.**
- **Reach:** 14 px touch reach for bend and grip presses, following the
  `onPinch` precedent.
- **Opt-in:** the binding opts in (`touchPress`), so Layout mode and
  object vertex drags keep today's touch behaviour.
- **Slop:** the viewer applies the 3 px slop before the first lifted drag,
  so a jittery tap can't become a 48 px move.
- **Grip offset:** measured from the un-lifted press. The grip jumps 48 px
  up at the start, like a control does.
- **Drag point:** `onPointerDrag` returns the placed point, which serves the
  stem, the loupe and F24, rather than adding a new callback.

## F24: a magnifier loupe for mouse drags

**Scope.** The D9b loupe during a **mouse** drag on desktop and tablet of
any of these:
- a control;
- a number;
- a bend;
- a grip;
- a course object's vertex or grip.

It's the same `editor-loupe` element and GL inset:
- centred on the dragged, snapped point;
- placed 16 px above the cursor, with no lift;
- flipped below the cursor at the top edge and clamped sideways;
- shown always while dragging (answer 2);
- gone on release, cancel or Escape.

The cursor keeps its crosshair at 1×, and the loupe takes no pointer
events.

Not in scope: body drags of objects, lasso, the gap tool, area drawing,
Layout-mode drags. This **reverses the recorded "Mouse drags get no
loupe"** (D9b, `course-editor.md` §10), so it's a deviation and gets a
decision-log row.

**Acceptance criteria.**
- **When it shows:** during a mouse drag of a control (`editorDragPos`), a
  number (`editorLabelDragPos`), a bend, a grip, or an object vertex or
  grip (the point `onPointerDrag` returns, from F23), `editor-loupe` is
  visible.
- **Its size and place:** 120 px at ×2.5, its bottom edge 16 px above the
  cursor. It flips to 16 px below when its top would leave the map box,
  and stays ≥ 4 px inside the box sideways.
- **When it goes:** on `mouseup`, a mouseleave cancel and Escape. A click
  without a drag shows nothing.
- **Touch is unchanged**, and the field-mode loupe test stays green.
- **Tests:**
  - `touch.test.ts`: `loupePlacement(point, box, liftPx = 0)` sits above
    the cursor, flips and clamps; the touch call is unchanged;
  - E2E, a new "F24" test in the F3 describe:
    - `dragHold` a control: the loupe is visible above it, and its circle
      differs from the same circle after `mouse.up` and holds purple;
    - `dragHold` a bend handle: the loupe is visible;
    - after release there's none.

**Steps.**
1. `touch.ts` + its test: `loupePlacement(at, box, liftPx = HANDLE_LIFT_PX)`.
2. `MapViewer.tsx`: compute `glLoupe` from `editorDragPos`,
   `editorLabelDragPos` or `toolDragPos`, with the mouse's cursor position
   tracked in container px during a drag. Render `editor-loupe` without
   the stem on a mouse drag, and set `data-pointer="mouse" | "touch"` on
   it for the tests.
3. E2E as above.
4. Docs:
   - `course-editor.md` §10 "Loupe" and the Tests table; remove the
     Pending "Mouse loupe";
   - `map-rendering.md` §5.5;
   - `features.md`, including fixing the stale legacy-loupe sentence at
     l. 79;
   - the decision-log row (a deviation from D9 / D9b);
   - SLICES.

## F25: live inspector leg metres during a bend or grip drag

**Scope.** These follow the F21 leg draft (live, and held after release)
while a bend or grip is dragged, the way a dragged control's leg labels
already do:
- the inspector's `editor-leg-meters` for the selected leg's row;
- the sequence footer's `km`;
- `totalMeters`.

The crossing point stays joined (`routedBends`). The status length already
follows the draft through the derivation, so it's left as is. **Also
(answer 3):** hide the White opacity field (`editor-leg-corridor-opacity`
and its hint `legCorridorOpacityHint`) in `LegSection.tsx`, keeping the
stored value and the API validation. Update `course-editor.md` §6.7 (the
field is hidden until the band can draw it) and remove the hint string in
en and sv.

**Acceptance criteria.**
- **During a drag:** while `shownLegDraft` is non-null and belongs to the
  selected leg, row `selectedLeg.itemIndex`'s metres are
  `legPolyline(from, draft.bends [through the crossing point], to)` ×
  scale, and the total sums it.
- **Without a draft:** identical to today.
- **After a drag:** a cancelled drag or Escape restores the stored metres.
  A held draft keeps the released metres until the list catches up.
- **The opacity field:** it isn't rendered. A stored `whiteOpacity` is
  kept on save: the corridor update spreads the stored corridor.
- **Tests:**
  - unit, `lib/editor/__tests__/sequence.test.ts`: a pure
    `legMeterInputs(rows, legOf, draft?)` substitutes the drafted bends
    for one row only, keeps the crossing-point routing, and with no draft

  - E2E, inside the F21 test (the held plain-leg drag): while held, row
    b's `editor-leg-meters` is longer than its chord value by the expected
    amount, and after Escape it's back;
  - the corridor E2E asserts that `editor-leg-corridor-opacity` is absent.

**Steps.**
1. The pure helper and its test (`lib/editor/sequence.ts`).
2. In `EditorPage.tsx`, the `legMeters` memo takes `shownLegDraft`. That
   means hoisting the `drawnLegBends` / `shownLegDraft` block above it: a

3. Hide the opacity field in `LegSection.tsx`, and remove the i18n hint
   (en and sv).
4. The E2E assertions.
5. Docs:

## F26 (deferred): a translucent corridor band

**What it means.** *White opacity* says how solid the white band outside a
corridor is. At 1 the band hides the map completely: the classic corridor
map, with white paper beside the corridor. At, say, 0.5 the map shows
faintly through the band, so the runner sees the surrounding terrain
dimmed but still readable, on screen and on the printed map alike. The
value is stored and validated, but every value draws as solid white,
because the band is a *cut-out* of the map (a clip region), not painted
white.

**Cost** (one full session, two with exact mixed opacities):
- **Stream:** `FillPass.opacity`.
- **Layer:** a band whose `whiteOpacity` is below 1 becomes a white fill
  with alpha in a new corridor-white colour row, above the map colours and
  below lower purple. Opacity 1 keeps the clip, bit-identical.
- **Backends:** the reference raster (`fill-opacity`), WebGL2 (a blended
  fill) and the PDF (ExtGState `/ca`).
- **Fixtures:** a new `course-corridor-translucent` scene with its
  goldens.
- **Caveat:** PDF transparency isn't PDF/X-1a.
- **Mixed opacities**, if it's built: the minimum over the banded legs
  wins (the planner's recommendation).

**Decided:** not now (answer 3). The field is hidden by F25 until this
row is built.
